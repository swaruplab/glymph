Where things stand, 17 November 2026, 8 am Eastern. Glymph 0.3.1 is frozen and has been run on all 54 libraries; the section "All tiers" just below shows it beside Glymph 0.3.0, CellBender 0.3.2 and the other tools. In short, against CellBender: about 19 times faster per library, lower per-cell error on 27 of 32 libraries with truth, level on removal for 10x cell lines never used in development, far ahead on nuclei, 1.4 points behind on BD Rhapsody, and it over-cleans very clean tissue (xenograft skin). The project is public at github.com/swaruplab/glymph, with a website at swaruplab.github.io/glymph and an overview deck in the repository's reports folder. The tier 1 and tier 2 sections further down are the first run (Glymph 0.2.0) and are kept for the record.

Cross-species counts are the ground truth here: in a human cell every mouse read is background, and the reverse. Cell-line mixtures carry only 1 to 3% contamination, far less than tissue nuclei, so a lead on this tier alone does not show a method is better on real tissue.

Genes masked from the species score, and why

In human Parse cells 68% of the "mouse" counts are on Gm42418 and 10% on Lars2, at the same level in wells that held only human cells: ribosomal RNA reads aligned to the wrong genome, not RNA from another cell. A gene is masked for every tool when it holds at least 0.5% of one species' cross-species counts and that share is more than ten times its share both in the other species' cells and in the empty partitions. The rule uses raw counts only. It also finds human IL6 and CSF1 in the xenograft mice, which carry those transgenes. Before the mask every tool removed about 20% on Parse.

Tier 2: mouse kidney, cells and nuclei

Five libraries pooled from two mouse subspecies (Janssen et al. 2023): three from cells and two from nuclei, with 3 to 35% background. The truth is the authors' own: for every cell, the background fraction read from the other subspecies' alleles. Proximal tubule (PT) cells make up most of each library, so PT marker counts in other cell types are almost all background.

How each score is defined
Evaluation cells
Barcodes at or above the order-of-magnitude count threshold in the raw matrix with at least 90% of counts from one species. Every tool is scored on the same barcodes.
Cross-species removed
Share of the other species' counts removed from evaluation cells, summed over cells. The per-cell median is the same share taken cell by cell.
Native loss
Same-species counts removed beyond the expected same-species background, as a share of native counts. The expectation scales each cell's cross-species counts by the species split of the empty droplets.
Same-species ratio
Same-species counts removed divided by that expectation. Above 1 means native counts were taken; well below 1 means same-species background was left in.
Entries above raw, non-integer entries
Count integrity. A cleaned entry must not exceed the raw entry and should stay a whole number.
False-positive budget
Extra counts a tool may remove beyond the background it expects, as a share of each gene's native counts. It buys removal at the price of native counts, so tools should be compared at similar values of same-species kept.
Per-cell error and rank agreement
Root mean squared log error and Kendall rank correlation between each cell's estimated and true contamination fraction.
Wall time, peak RAM, peak GPU
Wall time and peak resident memory of the whole command from /usr/bin/time; peak GPU memory from the tool's own report where it gives one.
